READ THIS FIRST — IT CHANGES HOW YOU USE THE LIST

The US bar is on RESIDENCE, not on nationality. A US company generally cannot
employ a person ordinarily resident in Iran (31 CFR Part 560). The same company
generally CAN employ an Iranian national who is a lawful resident of Germany,
Canada, or another country. This happens routinely.

So this is not a list of doors that are closed to you. It is a list of doors
that open the day you have residence somewhere else.

  Now              →  build relationships. Correspondence is not employment.
  After residency  →  apply properly. The whole list becomes live.

Sequence, not a wall:
    German Opportunity Card or Canadian residence  →  then this entire list.

WHAT YOU CAN DO WITH THIS LIST TODAY
================================================================================

You cannot be employed by these companies while resident in Iran. You can
correspond with people who work there. Those are different things, and the
second one is how the first one eventually happens.

So write to a founder or a head of brand — not to apply, but to send the
analysis. No ask, no CV, no request for a job. Something like:

    "I am not applying — I am not able to work in the US at the moment. I
    wrote this and thought you would find it interesting."

That email has a much higher reply rate than an application, because it does
not ask the recipient for anything. And a founder who remembers your name for
two years is worth more than a rejected application today.

Then, when you have residence in Germany or Canada, you are not a cold contact.
You are the person who sent them that thing about Nike's pricing.

That is the actual play here, and it is available to you right now.
