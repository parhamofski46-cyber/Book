# Nike CEO — Research & Outreach Kit
_Compiled 28 August 2026_

---

## 1. The situation (as of today)

- **The seat is not vacant.** Elliott Hill has been President & CEO of NIKE, Inc. since 14 October 2024, when he returned from retirement to replace John Donahoe. He is actively building his own team — as recently as **27 August 2026** Nike announced Jane Ewing joining as EVP & Chief Commercial Officer, effective 7 September.
- **Mark Parker** remains Executive Chairman of the Board.
- **David Denton** became EVP & CFO effective 17 August 2026 (also interim Corporate Controller from 4 September).
- **Treasure Heinle** is EVP & Chief People Officer.
- There is **no public CEO search, no job posting, and no application channel** for the role. Fortune 100 CEO seats are never advertised.

**Implication:** this is not an "application." It is a *candidacy positioning* exercise — getting your name into the succession bench that the Board and its retained search advisors maintain quietly, years before a seat opens.

---

## 2. How a Fortune-100 CEO is actually chosen

1. The **Nominating & Corporate Governance Committee** of the Board owns succession planning. Nike has a published charter for it.
2. The committee retains an **external search firm** — for public-company CEO succession this is almost always Spencer Stuart, Heidrick & Struggles, Russell Reynolds, Egon Zehnder, or Korn Ferry. Retained fees run ~33% of first-year comp, minimums $150k–$250k+, process 4–8 months.
3. The firm and the committee build a **leadership profile** ("what great looks like"), then map a longlist — mostly sitting CEOs, division presidents of peer companies, and internal successors.
4. Multi-stage interviews with the committee, then the full board. Investors (here: Phil Knight / Travis Knight, plus institutional holders) have informal weight.

**Nobody gets on that longlist by emailing careers@.** People get on it by being *known to the search partners* and to individual directors.

## 4. Why most such emails get zero replies

They ask before they give. They lead with the sender's biography instead of the company's problem. They are long. They request "a role" — an unanswerable ask — instead of "20 minutes," which is answerable.

**The email that gets read has four moves, in this order:**
1. **One sentence of intent** — say plainly what you want, no throat-clearing.
2. **A diagnosis with numbers** — prove you have read the 10-K, not the press release. This is the whole email. It is the only part that makes a reader think *this person is operating at our altitude.*
3. **Proof, in numbers** — P&L owned, scale run, turn delivered. Three lines. Adjectives are worthless here.
4. **A small, specific ask** — 20 minutes. Never "a job."

Length ceiling: **200 words.** Anything longer signals you are not senior.

## 5. The facts to build your diagnosis on (Nike FY2026, ended 31 May 2026)

- Q4 revenue **$10.97B, −1% reported / −4% currency-neutral**
- FY net income **$3.10B, −3%**; diluted EPS **$2.10, −3%**
- **Greater China Q4: $1.29B, −12% reported / −17% currency-neutral** — Nike Direct −14%, wholesale −19%, EBIT −20%; described as a multi-quarter marketplace reset
- Gross margin **49.2%, +890 bps** — but ~900 bps of that is a **one-time $986M IEEPA tariff recovery**. Strip it out and margin is roughly flat.
- Q4 diluted EPS $0.72 vs $0.14 — **$0.52 of it is that tariff benefit**
- Tariffs still guided as a **~$1.5B headwind** for the year
- Nike's average selling price in running: **$83** vs On's **$143** — the highest ASP in the top-10. Nike has re-passed On to become the **#3 running footwear seller** by volume.

**The sharpest available thesis:** Nike's problem is no longer demand or brand heat — it is **price authority**. The brand sells more pairs than On at 58% of On's price, and the FY26 margin line only looks healthy because of a tariff refund that will not repeat. The next chapter is won on ASP and on rebuilding China as a locally-designed business, not on volume.

## 6. Email 1 — the outside memo to the Board

> **In Gmail as a draft (28 Aug 2026), fully written, no placeholders left.** `To:` is empty on purpose — Nike publishes no board email address. Print it and post it to the Corporate Secretary at One Bowerman Drive. `Investor.Relations@nike.com` is the wrong function and will not route it.

**What changed and why.** The first version of this letter was a CEO candidacy resting on a three-line block of P&L proof. That block cannot be filled truthfully in this case, and a fabricated one would fail at the first question anyone asked. So the letter was rewritten to say plainly what is and is not there. It is now an outside strategic memo with a small, answerable ask at the end — a name of someone who takes questions. That is a reachable outcome. The CEO seat is not.

**Subject: Nike is winning units and losing price — an outside memo**

Full text lives in the Gmail draft. Structure:
1. One line of intent — outside the industry, one argument, five minutes.
2. The FY26 numbers, with the tariff benefit stripped out of the margin.
3. The $83 vs $143 ASP gap as the thesis.
4. What follows from it — price authority, not volume; China as a design problem, not a distribution one.
5. **An explicit statement of what the sender has not done.** This is the paragraph that makes the letter sendable rather than embarrassing.
6. Two zero-cost asks: take the analysis for free, and give me a name.

## 7. Email 2 — speculative application to On (finished, zero placeholders)

> **In Gmail as a draft.** Written end to end for **On** specifically. No brackets left to fill.

**Why this replaced the executive-search email.** That letter was built entirely on scale — revenue run, P&L owned, turn delivered. With none of that yet it is an empty form, and sending it burns a contact who would otherwise be reachable in ten years. This one trades on the analysis instead, which is real.

**Subject: The $83 problem is about to become On's problem**

**The move that makes it work.** It does not tell On that Nike is weak — On knows. It inverts the argument into On's own risk: the $143 ASP is On's whole equity story, and Nike has just proven it can win volume at half that price with the brand still impaired. Nike does not need to reach $143 to hurt On; it needs $110 with distribution it already owns. Going from $83 to $110 is a pricing decision. Defending $143 is a product and brand decision, and much harder. That is a non-obvious claim built only on verified numbers, and it is the reason someone would reply.

**To adapt it for adidas, Puma, ASICS, New Balance, Decathlon, JD Sports:** rewrite the fourth paragraph only. Everything else carries over. Each company needs its own version of "why this is your risk, not Nike's weakness" — a generic swap of the company name kills the email.

**No recipient address.** These brands hire through Workday portals, not inboxes. Use this text in the portal's cover-letter field, or send it as a LinkedIn message to a named person on the relevant team. Do not send it to a customer-service or general-info address.

## 7d. Where email actually works

Large brands (Nike, adidas, Puma, On, ASICS) hire through Workday portals and publish no application inbox. `info@` and `service@` addresses are customer service and never reach a strategy team. Small premium brands are the opposite: they publish a real address and a real person reads it — and they are the brands whose entire model rests on the price authority question, so the analysis lands.

**Verify before sending.** These addresses come from search-result summaries; the brand pages themselves were blocked by the network proxy in this session and could not be read directly. Open each careers page and confirm the address first.

**Montreal is not incidental.** Canada offers the most realistic relocation path of these jurisdictions (Express Entry, study permit) for an Iranian national, and Montreal is bilingual. The two highest-ranked targets are there, so a reply leads somewhere rather than into a visa dead end.

**Two finished drafts exist**, addressed and complete: norda and Ciele. Each carries a different fourth-paragraph argument — for norda, that On proves the premium position scales but Nike at $110 compresses it, so the defence has to be physical (bio-Dyneema, construction); for Ciele, that the $143 On buyer and the $50 cap buyer are one wallet, and identity is the only defence a giant cannot bundle its way past. Sending the same text to both would burn both.

## 7b. The hard constraint

Nike is a US company. Under the Iranian Transactions and Sanctions Regulations (31 CFR Part 560), US persons and US-organised entities are generally prohibited from transacting with, or employing, persons ordinarily resident in Iran, absent OFAC authorisation. Nike also has no Iran operations.

So: **no Nike role is available from Iran at any level** — not CEO, not an internship. The binding constraint is location and work authorisation, not the CV. Any real plan routes through relocation and a work permit first, and the sportswear job second. Non-US employers in the EU, UK, Japan, and the Gulf are the reachable set, and they still require sponsorship.

---

## 7c. The thing that compounds

Publish the ASP argument publicly, in English, under a real name — LinkedIn, Substack, anywhere indexable. It costs nothing, it is not gated by a CV, a visa, or a sanctions regime, and it is the one asset here that keeps working while its author sleeps. Several people have entered serious consideration for roles precisely this way. Do this before sending either email; it gives both of them something to point at.

## 8. The one-page memo to enclose

This is what actually earns the meeting. One page, five blocks, no design flourishes:

1. **What I see** — three bullets, all with numbers from §5.
2. **What I would do in 90 days** — three moves, each with the metric it moves.
3. **What I would stop** — one thing. Naming something to kill is the single strongest signal of operator judgment.
4. **What I would need** — one board-level ask.
5. **What I have done that is the same shape as this** — three lines.

---

## 9. Honest odds and the better-expected-value play

Cold outreach to a Fortune-100 board for an occupied CEO seat has a very low reply rate — realistically low single-digit percent for a *strong* letter from an already-credible operator, and effectively zero without a comparable-scale P&L record. That is the process working as designed, not a failure of the letter.

Higher-expected-value moves that use the same material:
- **Version B to five search firms.** They reply. They are paid to know who exists.
- **A Nike board seat** rather than the CEO seat — a real and much more open door, and directors are the pool CEOs get drawn from.
- **A senior operating role at Nike** (VP/GM level and above) posted at `jobs.nike.com` — the Hill era is visibly hiring, and internal candidates dominate CEO shortlists.
- **Publish the diagnosis.** A sharp public teardown of the ASP problem, under your name, reaches search partners and directors passively and permanently. Several people have entered CEO consideration exactly this way.
