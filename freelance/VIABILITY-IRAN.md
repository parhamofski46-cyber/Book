# Reality check — Iran-based, crypto-only payment

Compiled 2026-08-27.

## Payment

- **PayPal: not an option.** PayPal does not operate in Iran at all. This is not
  a restriction to work around — there is no service.
- **Crypto: newly harder.** On 24 August 2026 (three days ago) OFAC designated
  Iran's digital-asset sector, bringing it under the same sanctions umbrella as
  Iran's shipping, gold and aviation sectors. Unlike medical goods or personal
  remittances, crypto transfers have **no general-licence carve-out**. Exchanges
  and payment processors are actively de-risking Iran exposure right now.

The practical effect: any counterparty with US nexus — US company, US investor,
USD banking, Stripe, or a US-exposed exchange — will decline once residency is
known. This is a fact about their compliance department, not about your skills.

## Channels that actually fit the constraint

- **On-chain bounties** — Gitcoin and Opire settle in crypto directly on-chain.
  Note Algora does *not* fit: it settles via Stripe Connect, which excludes Iran.
- **Crypto-native / DAO teams** — jurisdiction-agnostic by design and already
  paying in stablecoins. web3.career and crypto.jobs list Python roles.
- **Non-US counterparties willing to contract directly** — EU, Turkey, UAE,
  Southeast Asia. Smaller companies, less compliance apparatus.
- **The regional market** — Iranian and Persian-language clients. Unglamorous,
  but no sanctions layer and no payment problem.
- **Actual relocation of residency** — Turkey, Georgia, UAE. This is the route
  most Iranian developers earning Western rates have taken. It is a real move,
  not a paperwork trick.

I'm not going to help construct a way to conceal residency from an employer —
that shifts legal exposure onto you and misrepresents you to the counterparty.
